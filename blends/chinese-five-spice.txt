Name: Chinese Five-Spice
Aka: five-spice powder, five spice, wu xiang fen
Cuisines: Chinese, Taiwanese, Vietnamese
Origin: China
Uses: Dry rub, Wet marinade or paste
Methods: Roast, Braise or stew, Fry
Classic: Pork, Duck, Chicken
Also: Beef, Tofu, Pork ribs
Tip: A little goes a long way and the clove and star anise are strong. Rub it into meat before roasting or add a pinch to a braise.
Typical version: The page names star anise, clove, Chinese cinnamon, Sichuan pepper and fennel seed as the common five. Fennel and star anise carry the licorice note.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-spice_powder
Confidence: medium
Doubt: The five spices are from the page, and the parts are my typical ratio. Clove is kept to half the others because it is so strong.

Ingredients:
2 parts Star anise
2 parts Cinnamon
2 parts Fennel seed
2 parts Pepper, Sichuan
1/2 part Clove
