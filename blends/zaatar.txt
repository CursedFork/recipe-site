Name: Za'atar
Aka: zaatar, zatar
Cuisines: Levantine, Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian, Jordanian, Israeli
Origin: Levant
Uses: Finishing sprinkle, Wet marinade or paste
Methods: Bake, Raw or finishing
Classic: Flatbread, Yogurt, Dips and spreads
Also: Vegetables
Tip: Sprinkle it dry over hummus, yogurt or roasted vegetables, or stir it into olive oil to make a spread for flatbread.
Typical version: A common home version, adapted from one tested recipe. The traditional herb is a wild relative of oregano (Origanum syriacum), which home cooks replace with thyme, oregano or marjoram, so oregano is marked optional here because many versions use only one of them. Lebanese versions lean on sumac, Palestinian versions often add caraway, and the amounts differ a great deal from cook to cook.
Careful: Some shop-bought blends add fillers such as wheat flour, so check the label if that matters to you.

Doubt: The proportions come from one recipe (thyme 2, oregano 1, sumac 1 1/2, toasted sesame 1, by tablespoon), doubled to avoid fractions. Its salt is a quarter teaspoon, shown here as a pinch. The pairings come from the traditional uses listed on the Wikipedia page.

Ingredients:
4 parts Thyme
3 parts Sumac
2 parts Sesame seed
2 parts Oregano (optional)
pinch Salt (optional)
