Name: Ras el Hanout
Aka: ras al hanout, rass el hanout
Cuisines: Moroccan, Algerian, Tunisian
Origin: Morocco
Uses: Dry rub, Cooking base or sauce
Methods: Braise or stew, Roast, Slow cook
Classic: Lamb, Chicken, Couscous
Also: Stews and braises, Carrots, Rice
Tip: Add it early to tagines and stews, or rub it into lamb and chicken. The name means the best spices in the shop, so no two blends match.
Typical version: A warm, floral mix of cumin, coriander, ginger, cinnamon, turmeric and paprika, with pepper, cardamom, allspice, nutmeg and clove. Many shop blends have more than a dozen spices.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ras_el_hanout
Confidence: medium
Doubt: The page lists the common ingredients and says there are usually over a dozen. The parts, and the choice of which to include, are my typical version. Rose petals, cubeb and grains of paradise appear in some versions and are left as optional.

Ingredients:
3 parts Cumin
3 parts Coriander
2 parts Ginger
2 parts Cinnamon
2 parts Paprika
2 parts Pepper, black
1 part Turmeric
1 part Cardamom
1 part Allspice
1/2 part Nutmeg
1/2 part Clove
1/2 part Chile, hot red: Cayenne (optional)
1/2 part Rose petals (optional)
1/2 part Grains of paradise (optional)
