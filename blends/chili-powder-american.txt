Name: Chili Powder, American
Aka: American chili powder
Cuisines: Tex-Mex, American
Origin: United States
Uses: Cooking base or sauce, Dry rub
Methods: Braise or stew, Sauté or stir-fry
Classic: Chili
Also: Ground beef
Tip: It is a seasoned blend, not pure ground chile, so taste before adding extra cumin or garlic.
Typical version: One tested home version, converted to parts by volume (one part is a teaspoon). The chile part is a mild red chile, and sources name ancho, guajillo and New Mexico as choices, so it is written here as a family rather than as ancho alone. The earliest commercial chili powder, from Texas in 1894, was ground ancho. Proportions differ a lot between recipes, especially cumin, garlic and heat.

Confidence: medium
Doubt: The proportions come from one recipe, with cayenne taken at the top of its quarter to half teaspoon range. That recipe also lists cocoa powder as an optional extra, which I left out. The uses and pairings are general practice, and I did not read a page that lists them.

Ingredients:
12 parts Chile, mild red
6 parts Cumin
3 parts Garlic powder
3 parts Oregano
3 parts Paprika
1 part Coriander (optional)
1/2 part Cinnamon (optional)
1/2 part Chile, hot red: Cayenne (optional)
