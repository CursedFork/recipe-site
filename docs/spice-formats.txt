SPICE AND BLEND FILE FORMATS (version 1, DRAFT awaiting approval)

Paste everything below this line into the instructions of any agent that writes spice or blend files.
================================================================================

You write plain .txt files for a spice library website. There are two kinds: one file per SPICE in /spices and one file per BLEND in /blends. A program reads them at build time. If a file breaks any rule below, the whole site build fails and names the file and the problem, so follow the rules exactly.

GENERAL RULES

1. Plain text, UTF-8. Each line is "Field: value". A field name is exactly as written below, with a capital first letter.
2. All the single-line fields come first. Then ONE blank line. Then, in a blend file only, the "Ingredients:" section.
3. Leave out an optional field completely when you have nothing to put in it. Do not write an empty field.
4. Do not invent anything. Every blend must be a real, documented blend, and every fact must come from a source you actually read. If you cannot find a source, leave the entry out or mark it Confidence: low and say why in Doubt.
5. Write every description in your own words. Never copy sentences from a website. Ingredient lists and ratios are facts and may be recorded.
6. Use generic names, never brand names.
7. Make no health claims. Add a Careful line only where a reliable source gives a real safety point.
8. Pairings are traditional suggestions, not rules. Leave out a pairing you are unsure of rather than guessing.
9. Use "to" for number ranges and plain commas and colons. Do not use long dashes.

FILENAMES

The filename is the web address, and it must equal the slug of the Name:
lowercase; accents removed; apostrophes removed; each run of anything that is not a letter or digit becomes one hyphen; no hyphen at the start or end.
  Pepper, black: Tellicherry   ->  pepper-black-tellicherry.txt
  Za'atar                       ->  zaatar.txt
  Chile, mild red: Ancho        ->  chile-mild-red-ancho.txt

NAMING A SPICE

Name = Base, kind: Variety
  Base    the word a cook looks up first (Pepper, Chile, Cinnamon, Cumin)
  kind    the broad type, after a comma (black, white, mild red)
  Variety the named cultivar or origin, after a colon (Tellicherry, Ancho)
A spice with nothing to group under it is just its name: Clove, Cumin. Use the singular and sentence case.
If a spice has a Family, its Name MUST be the family's Name, then a colon and a space, then its variety:
  Family: Pepper, black     Name: Pepper, black: Tellicherry

================================================================================
SPICE FILE
================================================================================

Name:            required. Follows the naming rule. Must be unique.
Aka:             optional. Comma list of other names, spellings and plurals. Each word must be unique across ALL spice files and must not equal any other spice's Name.
Category:        required. Exactly one of the CATEGORIES below.
Family:          optional. The exact Name of another spice file (the "umbrella"). One level only.
Form:            required. Comma list of: whole, ground, flakes, leaves, granules.
Origin:          required. Where it TRADITIONALLY comes from, not where it is grown today. Countries or regions, comma separated.
Cuisines:        required. Comma list from the CUISINES list. Use Global for a spice used everywhere.
Flavor:          required. Comma list of plain words.
Power:           required, whole number 1 to 5 (see POWER). NOT used for the categories Salts and sugars or Other ingredients: leave it out there.
Heat:            whole number 0 to 5 (see HEAT). REQUIRED for the category Chiles. Leave it out for everything else unless a source gives a real figure.
Allergen:        optional. One of the ALLERGENS below.
Tip:             required. One short sentence of usage advice.
Pairs with:      required. Comma list from the FOODS list (a group name or an item name).
Typical dishes:  optional. Comma list of free text.
Careful:         optional. One sentence, only for a real safety point from a reliable source.
Source:          required, and may be repeated, one URL per line. At least one.
Confidence:      required. high, medium or low.
Doubt:           optional. One or two sentences saying what you are unsure of. Required whenever Confidence is not high.

A spice is ONE entry whatever its form. Ancho pods and ancho powder are the same entry.

================================================================================
BLEND FILE
================================================================================

Name:            required. The common English name. Unique. A regional variant that really differs may add a comma and a qualifier: Chili Powder, American.
Aka:             optional. Comma list. Unique across all blend files.
Cuisines:        required. Comma list from CUISINES. The first is the primary cuisine.
Origin:          required. Where it traditionally comes from. Countries or regions.
Uses:            required. Comma list from USES.
Methods:         required. Comma list from METHODS.
Classic:         required. Comma list of foods from FOODS that it is traditionally paired with. At least one.
Also:            optional. Comma list of foods that it also works with. A food may not be in both Classic and Also.
Tip:             required. One short sentence on how to use it.
Typical version: required. One to three sentences saying this is ONE typical version, not the only correct one, and noting how real versions differ. If sources disagree, use the most common version and say so here.
Careful:         optional. One sentence, only for a real safety point from a reliable source.
Source:          required, repeatable, one URL per line.
Confidence:      required. high, medium or low.
Doubt:           optional, but required whenever Confidence is not high.

(one blank line)

Ingredients:
<one ingredient per line>

Allergens are NEVER typed on a blend. The site works them out from its ingredients.

INGREDIENT LINES

  <amount> <spice name>
  <amount> <spice name> (optional)

  amount     a number then the word part or parts: "8 parts", "1 part", "1/2 part", "2 1/4 parts", "1.5 parts". Parts are by VOLUME and assume ground spices. Or the single word "pinch", or the two words "to taste".
  spice name the Name (or an Aka) of a spice file, exactly. Commas and colons are part of the name.
  (optional) means it is nice to have and never blocks the blend. Anything without it is REQUIRED.

Write a FAMILY name (an umbrella spice) when any member would do, for example "Pepper, black". Write a VARIETY name only when exactly that spice is needed, for example "Chile, hot red: Cayenne".
Convert recipes to parts. Keep the ratio faithful to the source. Round only if you must, and say so in Typical version.
Every spice named in a blend must exist as a spice file. If it does not, say so; do not invent it.

================================================================================
SCALES
================================================================================

POWER: how easily it overwhelms a dish. Judge by the typical amount in a dish for four people.
  1  a tablespoon or more          mild and forgiving       (sweet paprika, parsley, sesame)
  2  two teaspoons to a tablespoon  gentle                  (ancho, coriander, thyme)
  3  about a teaspoon              moderate                 (cumin, black pepper, oregano)
  4  a quarter to a half teaspoon   use sparingly            (white pepper, nutmeg, cardamom, cayenne)
  5  a pinch to an eighth teaspoon  a pinch goes a long way  (clove, asafoetida)
These are editorial judgments. If you are unsure between two levels, pick one and set Confidence to low with a Doubt line.

HEAT: capsaicin heat, by Scoville range. Use a published figure.
  0 none    1 up to 2,500    2 from 2,500 to 15,000    3 from 15,000 to 50,000    4 from 50,000 to 100,000    5 above 100,000

--- /spices/clove.txt ---
Name: Clove
Aka: cloves, whole cloves, ground cloves, ground clove
Category: Warm spices
Form: whole, ground
Origin: Indonesia
Cuisines: Indonesian, Indian, Dutch
Flavor: warm, sweet, sharply aromatic, almost medicinal
Power: 5
Tip: A very little goes a long way, so start with one or two whole cloves in a pot or a pinch of ground. Fish whole cloves out before serving.
Pairs with: Ham, Curry, Apples and pears
Typical dishes: baked ham, curries, pumpkin pie spice, speculaas, hot spiced drinks
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clove
Confidence: high

--- /blends/zaatar.txt ---
Name: Za'atar
Aka: zaatar, zatar
Cuisines: Levantine, Lebanese, Syrian, Palestinian, Jordanian, Israeli
Origin: Levant
Uses: Finishing sprinkle, Wet marinade or paste
Methods: Bake, Raw or finishing
Classic: Flatbread, Yogurt, Dips and spreads
Also: Vegetables
Tip: Sprinkle it dry over hummus, yogurt or roasted vegetables, or stir it into olive oil to make a spread for flatbread.
Typical version: A common home version, adapted from one tested recipe. The traditional herb is a wild relative of oregano (Origanum syriacum), which home cooks replace with thyme, oregano or marjoram, so oregano is marked optional here because many versions use only one of them. Lebanese versions lean on sumac, Palestinian versions often add caraway, and the amounts differ a great deal from cook to cook.
Careful: Some shop-bought blends add fillers such as wheat flour, so check the label if that matters to you.
Source: https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/9962-zaatar
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Za%27atar
Confidence: medium
Doubt: The proportions come from one recipe (thyme 2, oregano 1, sumac 1 1/2, toasted sesame 1, by tablespoon), doubled to avoid fractions. Its salt is a quarter teaspoon, shown here as a pinch. The pairings come from the traditional uses listed on the Wikipedia page.

Ingredients:
4 parts Thyme
3 parts Sumac
2 parts Sesame seed
2 parts Oregano (optional)
pinch Salt (optional)
